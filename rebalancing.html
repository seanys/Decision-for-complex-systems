With conventional multi-agent reinforcement learning, every district learns from the same reward, in which its own effect shrinks as the city grows.

How DG-PG trains the districts, step by step

Once the demand pattern is known, a fluid model of the fleet tells how many robotaxis each district needs so that departures and arrivals balance everywhere. Since a robotaxi sent now arrives up to 20 minutes later, what matters is where the fleet will be 20 minutes ahead. Could this knowledge be internalized into how the districts are trained?

1
Referenceoffline, centralized
x~j(t)=∑k(λkjp+rkjp)τkj+bj,p=pattern at t+20 min

Robotaxis that should be in or on their way to district j: λp are the ride rates of the pattern, rp the empty-car flows that balance every district's departures and arrivals in the fluid model of Pavone et al. (2012), τ the travel times and bj an idle buffer.

2
Guidanceper district
gi(t)=12∑j(xj(t)−x~j(t)s)2−12∑j(xj−i(t)−x~j(t)s)2

xj(t): robotaxis in district j 20 minutes after this dispatch, counting the idle ones kept there and those arriving within 20 minutes; x−i: the same had district i kept all its idle robotaxis; s: mean requests per district and 5 minutes. Negative when district i's dispatch brings the fleet closer to the reference.

3
Learning signalper district
A^i(t)=(1−α)A(t)−αg^i(t)

Shared advantage A of the reward r = −(riders without a car + 0.1 × empty robotaxi-steps) / 100, combined with the normalized guidance; α = 0.9.

4
Policy updateinternalizes the reference
∇θJ≈∑t∑iA^i(t)∑jnij(t)∇θlog⁡pij(o(t))

District i sends each idle robotaxi to district j with probability pij, and nij robotaxis go there; the reference and the guidance are not needed at run time.

At its core, DG-PG uses prior knowledge of the system, in the form of a reference state, to guide the training of the districts and give each district a clear optimization signal. Through training, each district internalizes in its policy where to send its idle robotaxis as demand shifts across the city.
